# NawaHub ERP — Functional & UI/UX Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Scope:** full platform · **Deployment model:** single-company, single legal entity
**Method:** source trace + live-app execution against a seeded throwaway Postgres + multi-reviewer cross-check
**Nature:** audit only — **no application code was modified.** The only file added is this report.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Architectural Health Checklist

NawaHub is a substantially more mature system than its "mini-erp" package name suggests. It has a real
double-entry posting engine, FIFO/AVCO cost layering with advisory-lock concurrency control, an approval
engine with segregation-of-duties checks, historical-rate multi-currency, period close with balance
snapshots, and a genuinely excellent bilingual (EN/AR) RTL implementation. The frontend is built on a
disciplined set of shared primitives rather than copy-paste pages.

The problems are not architectural drift. They are **a small number of deep correctness defects**
concentrated in four places: the period-close carry-forward chain, the transaction boundary between
business documents and their journal entries, multi-currency amount handling, and record-level
authorization. Each is a localized fix in a well-structured codebase — but until they are fixed, the
system reports incorrect financial statements and does not enforce the data-access scoping it appears
to offer.

### The headline finding

**Closing an accounting period silently erases all prior-period history from every financial statement —
and the system still reports `balanced: true`.** This was reproduced end-to-end against the running app,
not inferred from reading code.

|                                                    | Total Assets   | Total Equity    | Owner's Capital row | self-check       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | --------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| Baseline, no periods closed                        | 694,144.00     | 211,400.00      | 500,000.00          | `balanced: true` |
| After closing 2026-06 (2026-01 still open)         | **194,144.00** | **−288,600.00** | **absent**          | `balanced: true` |
| After closing the unbroken chain 2026-01 … 2026-06 | 694,144.00     | 211,400.00      | 500,000.00          | `balanced: true` |

Exactly 500,000 — the January capital injection — disappears. The trigger is **any gap in the closed-period
chain, including a month with no transactions at all**, because `closePeriod()` carries opening balances
only from `priorPeriod(period)` (`backend/src/accounting/period-close.service.ts:54-59`). Close January
and June but not February–May, and January's history is gone from the balance sheet, the trial balance
and the P&L. The `balanced` flag cannot catch it: dropping a balanced entry leaves the remainder balanced.

**Verification baseline:** `npm --prefix backend test` → **12 suites, 121 tests, all passing** (127 s).
The defects below are therefore _not_ regressions — they are gaps the suite never covered.

### Priority order

1. **P0 — Record-level authorization is inert** on finance, procurement, sales and leads. A user granted
   `invoices:view:own` sees _every_ invoice. This is the fastest-exploitable defect and the cheapest to fix.
2. **P0 — Period close chain** (proven). Do not let customers close periods until fixed.
3. **P0 — Multi-currency**: unknown-rate 1:1 fallback and cross-currency payment relief.
4. **P0 — Document/journal atomicity** across invoices, bills, expenses, PO receipt.
5. **P1 — RBAC registry gap**: Leads, Accounting-read, Warehouses, Activities cannot be granted to any non-superadmin role.
6. **P1 — Inventory adjustments post no GL**, so the Inventory Asset account drifts from the subledger forever.
7. **P1 — Accessibility**: recolour accent themes and borders before any WCAG commitment.

**Second headline finding.** The `finance` / `procurement` / `sales` / `leads` resource names used by 25
scope call sites **do not exist in the permission registry**, and `resolveScope()` fails _open_ — so the
own/team scoping feature silently does nothing across most of the transactional surface. Two independent
reviewers reached the RBAC problem from opposite directions: one from the Roles UI, one from the services.

## 2. Comprehensive ERP Functional Audit

Severity rubric follows the repo's own `AGENTS.md`: **P0** silent corruption / money wrong / auth bypass ·
**P1** user-visible breakage or compliance violation · **P2** edge-case or perf · **P3** hygiene.
Every finding cites `file:line`. Findings proven by running the app are marked **PROVEN**.

### 2.1 Financial Management / General Ledger

#### [P0] Period close silently drops all history before a gap in the chain — **PROVEN**

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/period-close.service.ts:54-59`, consumed by `backend/src/accounting/statements.service.ts:21-59`
- **What:** `closePeriod()` seeds opening balances only from `priorPeriod(period)`. If that exact month has no `AccountBalance` rows, opening silently becomes `0`.
- **Failure scenario:** Ledger has activity in Jan (500,000 capital) and Jun. Close Jun. Feb–May were never closed, so they hold no snapshot rows → Jun opens at 0. Balance sheet drops from 694,144 to 194,144 assets and equity goes to −288,600, while still reporting `balanced: true`. Reproduced above.
- **Aggravating factor:** `reopenPeriod()` (`:99-106`) clears `closedAt` but leaves the rows, and re-closing an earlier period never recomputes later snapshots — so a correction to January never propagates into an already-closed February.
- **Fix sketch:** Require sequential close (reject if any earlier period is open); or seed opening from the latest snapshot ≤ period rather than exactly `priorPeriod`. Recompute or invalidate all later snapshots when an earlier period is reopened or re-closed.

#### [P0] Closing a period with no activity does not lock it — **PROVEN**

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/period-close.service.ts:21-24`, `:65-92`
- **What:** "Closed" is inferred from the existence of `AccountBalance` rows. A period with no prior balances and no activity creates zero rows, so `isPeriodClosed()` stays `false`.
- **Failure scenario:** `POST /api/accounting/periods/2027-06/close` returned `{"accounts":0}`; `GET /periods` returned `[]` (not listed as closed at all); a manual journal dated 2027-06-15 then posted successfully as `JE-0031`. The UI reports a successful close for a period that is not locked.
- **Fix sketch:** Store period state in its own table/row, independent of account snapshots.

#### [P0] Unknown exchange rate silently posts at 1:1

- **Where:** `backend/src/common/currency.service.ts:42` — `return rate ? amount * rate : amount;`
- **What:** When no `ExchangeRate` row exists for a currency, the amount passes through unconverted. The docstring frames this as deliberate ("so totals never silently collapse"), which is defensible for _reporting aggregates_ but not for _posting money to the ledger_.
- **Failure scenario:** A USD 1,000 invoice with no USD rate on file posts AR as **EGP 1,000** instead of ~EGP 50,000. No warning, no error, and the entry balances perfectly because every leg is wrong by the same factor. A rate of literal `0` is also falsy and takes the same path.
- **Fix sketch:** Split the API — keep pass-through for dashboards, but add a strict `toBaseOrThrow()` used by `PostingService` that rejects a missing rate.

#### [P0] Cross-currency payments relieve AR/AP with the wrong quantity

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/posting.service.ts:390-392` and `:592-594`
- **What:** The same numeric `amount` is treated simultaneously as payment-currency units (cash leg) and document-currency units (AR/AP leg).
- **Failure scenario:** A USD 100 invoice booked at 50 EGP/USD is paid with EGP 5,000. Cash is debited EGP 5,000, but AR is credited `toBase(5000, 'USD', 50)` = **EGP 250,000**, and the difference is booked as a fabricated **EGP 245,000 FX loss**. `recordPayment` (`backend/src/finance/invoices.service.ts:436, 447`) explicitly accepts a payment currency different from the invoice, so this path is reachable. Vendor-bill payments mirror it.
- **Fix sketch:** Carry the applied amount in document currency separately from the tender amount; use tender for cash, applied-at-historical-rate for AR/AP.

#### [P1] Automated reversals bypass the closed-period lock

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/posting.service.ts:232-269` (`reverseEntry`), reached via `reverse()` `:275` and `reverseLive()` `:312`
- **What:** `reverseById()` (`:301`) checks `periods.isDateLocked()`; `reverse()` and `reverseLive()` do not. The asymmetry shows the check was known to be needed.
- **Failure scenario:** Eleven call sites reach `reverseLive()` — invoice reject/void (`invoices.service.ts:400, 416`), payment delete/edit (`:482, 531`), vendor-bill edit/void (`vendor-bills.service.ts:293, 309`), bill-payment delete (`:385`), expense edit (`expenses.service.ts:233, 250`). None passes a date, so the mirror is dated `new Date()`. If the **current** period is closed, the reversal posts into it anyway.
- **Secondary defect:** because the mirror is dated today while the original keeps its own date, editing a document from a prior period leaves the original _and_ its correction in the old period and the reversal in the current one — the old period is overstated by one document until someone reads both.
- **Fix sketch:** Move the lock check into `reverseEntry()` so every path inherits it.

#### [P1] Journal number sequence is consumed outside the caller's transaction

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/posting.service.ts:204` and `:237` → `backend/src/number-sequence/number-sequence.service.ts:18`
- **What:** `nextNumber()` always runs on the root Prisma connection, never the caller's `tx`.
- **Failure scenario:** Any rollback after the number is drawn leaves a permanent gap in the `JE-` sequence. `withSerializableRetry` (used by `recordPayment` and `editPayment`) makes this systematic: **every serialization retry burns another journal number**. An idempotent duplicate post also burns one before hitting the P2002 path at `:221`. Gapless journal numbering is a statutory requirement in many jurisdictions.
- **Fix sketch:** Accept a transaction client in `nextNumber()`; define an explicit policy for voided numbers.

#### [P2] Statements scan the full ledger when no period has been closed

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/statements.service.ts:36-55`
- **What:** With no closed period, `boundary` is null, `liveFrom` is undefined, and the `groupBy` aggregates **every journal line since inception** on every trial balance / balance sheet / P&L / cash-flow request. The docstring's "never the whole history" holds only after the first close.

#### [P2] No fiscal-year close; retained earnings never crystallise

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/statements.service.ts:132-138`
- **What:** Net income is recomputed from all income/expense accounts since inception and injected as a synthetic `3900 Retained Earnings (current)` row. There is no year-end closing entry, so P&L accounts accumulate across fiscal years forever and prior-year profit is never locked into equity.

#### [P2] "Cash flow" is a cash-account delta, not a cash-flow statement

- **Where:** `backend/src/accounting/statements.service.ts:157-183`
- **What:** Reports net change per cash GL account with no operating / investing / financing classification. Fine as a treasury view; it should not be labelled a cash-flow statement for reporting or audit purposes.

#### Missing capability — Fixed-asset lifecycle

No model exists for asset registers, depreciation schedules, or disposals (`schema.prisma` has no asset
entity). `defaultInventoryAdjustment` is the closest mapping and is itself unused. Required before the
platform can claim complete financial management. Also absent: recurring journals, budget-vs-actual.

#### Refuted hypotheses (checked, found sound)

- **Tax-inclusive vendor bills do _not_ unbalance the GL.** `calcTotals()` (`backend/src/finance/finance.math.ts:53-84`) back-calculates net out of the gross line and guarantees `total = subtotal + tax` in both modes, so `Dr subtotal + Dr tax / Cr total` balances exactly.
- **FX conversion does not break the balance check.** `toBase()` performs no rounding, so per-leg conversion stays distributive well inside `POST_TOLERANCE`.
- **Concurrency on payments is handled properly** — `withSerializableRetry` + `Serializable` isolation + re-fetch inside the transaction (`invoices.service.ts:438-467`), with cross-currency overpayment converted at the invoice's recorded rate.

### 2.2 Supply Chain & Inventory

The costing engine is the strongest single component in the codebase and should be preserved as-is.

**Credits (verified):** transaction-scoped advisory lock keyed on `(product, warehouse)` correctly covers
the first-ever movement where a row lock cannot (`inventory.service.ts:146-148`); negative stock is
blocked (`:157-161`); FIFO returns re-enter the queue at the _original outbound's_ date rather than the
back (`:225-233`); transfers are atomic within one transaction (`:255-265`); reorder levels and a
`lowStock()` view exist (`:299`, `:351-361`).

#### [P1] Manual stock adjustments post no GL entry at all

- **Where:** `backend/src/inventory/inventory.service.ts:379-402`; `defaultInventoryAdjustment` is declared (`posting.service.ts:23`), seeded to `5300` (`chart-of-accounts.seed.ts:65`) and exposed in Settings (`frontend/src/pages/settings/GlMappingSettings.tsx:25`) — but **referenced by no posting code anywhere**.
- **Failure scenario:** A recount writes off 100 units at EGP 50. `StockItem.totalValue` drops by EGP 5,000; the Inventory Asset GL account does not move. Every write-off, damage record and recount widens a permanent, silent gap between the balance sheet and the stock subledger.
- **Fix sketch:** Post `Dr/Cr Inventory Adjustment ↔ Inventory Asset` from `adjust()`, inside the movement transaction.

#### [P1] Stock return restores inventory outside the GL transaction

- **Where:** `backend/src/inventory/inventory.service.ts:48-63`
- **What:** `applyMovement()` runs in its own transaction and commits; `postCogsReversal()` is then called with `undefined` for `tx`.
- **Failure scenario:** A return restores 5 units worth EGP 500; the COGS reversal then throws (missing mapping, or a closed period) and inventory and GL sit EGP 500 apart with no failed-state record.

#### [P2] FIFO layer consumption has no deterministic tie-break

- **Where:** `backend/src/inventory/inventory.service.ts:89-92` — `orderBy: { receivedAt: 'asc' }` only
- **What:** Layers sharing a `receivedAt` (same-millisecond receipt, or returns restored to an identical timestamp) consume in arbitrary order, so COGS for the same sale is not reproducible. Order by `(receivedAt, id)`.

### 2.3 Procurement

#### [P1] No three-way match exists

- **Where:** `backend/src/procurement/**` — a search for `receivedQty`, `billedQty`, `threeWay`, or tolerance handling returns nothing.
- **What:** `receive()` (`purchase-orders.service.ts:41`) receives the **entire ordered quantity, once**. There is no partial receipt, no over-receipt tolerance, and no check that a vendor bill's quantities or amounts match what was actually received.
- **Failure scenario:** A supplier ships 80 of 100 units and bills for 100. Nothing in the system objects; the bill posts in full to AP and GRNI never clears. This is the core procurement control and its absence is a material weakness for an ERP.

#### [P1] PO receipt is non-atomic and can become permanently stuck

- **Where:** `backend/src/procurement/purchase-orders.service.ts:41-69`
- **What:** Each line's `applyMovement()` commits in its own transaction, `postGrni()` is called with no `tx`, and the `status: 'received'` update happens only after the loop.
- **Failure scenario:** A 5-line PO fails on line 3 (missing GRNI mapping). Lines 1–2 are committed as stock and GL. The PO status was never updated, so it still looks unreceived — but retrying throws `"Purchase order has already been received"` because the idempotency probe at `:47` finds line 1's movement. The PO is now unreceivable and un-completable without direct DB surgery.

#### [P0] A second, divergent receipt path creates uncosted stock

- **Where:** `backend/src/procurement/purchase-orders.service.ts:210-229` (`updateStatus`)
- **What:** Setting status to `received` moves stock with **no `unitCost`** and **no GRNI posting**, under `refType: 'PurchaseOrder'` — while `receive()` uses `refType: 'po-receipt'`.
- **Failure scenario:** The status endpoint receives 10 units valued at the current average (possibly 0). `receive()` does not recognise that reference, so it receives the same 10 units _again_ at the real cost. Stock is doubled and only half is on the GL.

### 2.4 CRM & Sales

#### [P0] Quote → invoice conversion creates an approved invoice with no GL or stock effect

- **Where:** `backend/src/finance/quotes.service.ts:235-285`
- **What:** Conversion writes the invoice and its line items directly via `tx.invoice.create`, setting `approvalStatus: invoiceApprovalEnabled ? 'pending' : 'approved'`, and never calls `PostingService` or `InventoryService`.
- **Failure scenario:** With invoice approvals disabled (the default), converting an EGP 1,140 quote produces an **approved** invoice that records no AR, no revenue, no tax, no stock movement and no COGS. Revenue is simply missing from the books.
- **Fix sketch:** Route conversion through the same transactional creation path `InvoicesService` uses.

#### [P0] Pending invoices deplete stock and post COGS before approval

- **Where:** `backend/src/finance/invoices.service.ts:217-231`
- **What:** The stock/COGS loop runs unconditionally on create, regardless of `approvalStatus`.
- **Failure scenario:** A pending invoice for 10 units at EGP 100 immediately reduces stock by 10 and books EGP 1,000 COGS. Rejection (`:400`) reverses only the `Invoice` journal — the `StockCogs` entry and the stock movement are never touched, so a rejected sale permanently consumed inventory.

#### [P0] Editing or deleting an approved document leaves its postings live

- **Where:** `backend/src/finance/invoices.service.ts:262, 278, 339, 352`; `vendor-bills.service.ts:225, 462`; `expenses.service.ts:168, 262`
- **What:** Financial edits reset the document to pending without reversing the live entry, and re-approval posts again under a versioned `sourceId`. Soft deletion performs no reversal at all.
- **Failure scenario:** An approved invoice posts AR 114; its lines are edited to 228 and re-approved; AR is now **342**. Separately, deleting a posted invoice hides it from the UI while AR, revenue, tax, stock depletion and COGS all remain in the ledger.

#### [P1] Document approval commits before the GL post, with no shared transaction

- **Where:** `invoices.service.ts:367, 385`; `vendor-bills.service.ts:160, 187, 246, 277, 422, 455`; `expenses.service.ts:113, 145, 198, 217`
- **What:** Approval state commits, then posting is invoked without that transaction.
- **Failure scenario:** An invoice becomes `approved`; posting then fails on a missing AR mapping. Retrying returns early because the invoice is already approved — leaving an approved document with no journal entry, permanently.

_Missing capability:_ contract lifecycle management (renewals, terms, obligations) has no model.

### 2.5 Human Capital & Payroll — **not implemented**

No `Employee`, `Payroll`, `Compensation`, `LeaveRequest` or benefits models exist. `User.reportsTo`
provides an org tree (already used well by `VisibilityService`) and is the natural anchor point.

**Blueprint.** Add `Employee` (1:1 with `User`, carrying hire date, contract type, cost centre),
`CompensationPlan` (effective-dated), `PayrollRun` (period, status, approval), `PayslipLine` (earning/
deduction/employer-contribution typed), and `LeaveBalance`. Integrate at exactly one seam: a `PayrollRun`
posting `Dr Salary Expense / Dr Employer Contributions / Cr Net Pay Payable / Cr Tax & Social Insurance
Payable` through the existing `PostingService.post()`, reusing `costCenterId` on `PostLine` for analytic
allocation. Egypt-specific: progressive income-tax brackets and social-insurance ceilings must be
effective-dated tables, not constants. **Do not start this before the P0 GL defects are fixed** — payroll
would inherit every one of them.

### 2.6 Operations & Manufacturing — **not implemented**

No BOM, work order, routing, or capacity model exists.

**Blueprint.** `BillOfMaterials` + `BomLine` (component product, qty-per, scrap %), `WorkOrder`
(status, planned/actual qty, dates), `WorkOrderComponent` (issued qty), and `WorkCenter` (capacity/
calendar) for a later MRP/CRP layer. The integration seam is inventory, and it is already well suited:
component issue is an outbound `applyMovement` consuming `StockCostLayer`s, finished-goods receipt is an
inbound movement whose `unitCost` is the summed consumed component cost plus applied labour/overhead —
which is exactly what `consumeFifo()` already returns. GL: `Dr WIP / Cr Inventory` on issue,
`Dr Finished Goods / Cr WIP` on completion, with variances to a dedicated account.

### 2.7 Reporting, Analytics & Audit

- Statements are correct **only** under an unbroken closed-period chain (§2.1).
- `AccountBalance` indexes lead with `accountId` while the hot statement and lock queries filter on
  `period` / `closedAt` (`schema.prisma:1442`, `period-close.service.ts:22`, `statements.service.ts:23`).
  Add `(period, closedAt)`.
- Period close performs one awaited `upsert` per account inside the transaction
  (`period-close.service.ts:68-92`) — 10,000 accounts means 10,000 sequential writes holding the lock.
- Reconciliation loads every approved invoice into memory and scans the full ledger
  (`reconciliation.service.ts:47-76`).
- A `Log` model and `TimelineEvent` exist and are used, but GL mutations are not consistently captured —
  a period close/reopen (which, per §2.1, can change every reported number) leaves no audit record.

### 2.8 Security & Access Control

**Credits (verified):** `JwtAuthGuard` + `PermissionGuard` are applied at controller level with per-route
`@RequirePermission`; the manager subtree is resolved with a single recursive CTE rather than N+1 queries
(`backend/src/common/visibility.service.ts:33-45`); self-approval is blocked in six modules; `/auth` routes
carry tight per-route throttles (`auth.controller.ts:30, 52`).

The authentication layer is sound. The **authorization** layer is where the problems are.

#### [P0] Record-level scoping is silently inert across most of the app — **VERIFIED**

- **Where:** `backend/src/common/visibility.service.ts:15-22` (the `return 'all'` fallback) and `:51-61` (`ownershipWhere`), consumed with non-existent resource names throughout `backend/src/finance/**`, `backend/src/procurement/**`, `backend/src/reports/**`
- **What:** `resolveScope()` returns `'all'` when no permission matches — a deliberate fail-open, justified in its comment by "the permission guard already authorised the request". But the resource names callers pass do not match the permission vocabulary. Measured across the codebase:

  | Resource name passed to `ownershipWhere`                       | Call sites | Exists in `PERMISSION_REGISTRY`?                            |
  | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
  | `finance`                                                      | 14         | ❌ **no** (registry uses `invoices`, `quotes`)              |
  | `leads`                                                        | 7          | ❌ **no**                                                   |
  | `procurement`                                                  | 3          | ❌ **no** (registry uses `purchase-orders`, `vendor-bills`) |
  | `sales`                                                        | 1          | ❌ **no** (registry uses `sales-orders`)                    |
  | `deals`, `contacts`, `quotes`, `tasks`, `expenses`, `projects` | 34         | ✅ yes                                                      |

- **Failure scenario:** An administrator grants a junior a narrowly-scoped `invoices:view:own` role. The
  request passes `PermissionGuard`. `InvoicesService` then calls `ownershipWhere(user, 'finance', …)`;
  no `finance:*` permission exists, so `resolveScope` falls through to `'all'`, `ownershipWhere` returns
  `{}`, and `GET /api/finance/invoices` returns **every invoice in the company**. The same holds for
  purchase orders, vendor bills, sales orders and leads. The scoping feature appears configured and does
  nothing.
- **Fix sketch:** Make the fallback fail _closed_, and derive one canonical resource vocabulary shared by
  the registry, the guards and the services so this class of drift becomes impossible.

#### [P0] Write operations authorized by read-only permissions

- **Where:** `backend/src/activities/activities.controller.ts:12-16` (class-level `@RequirePermission('activities:view')` covering `@Post`, `@Put`, `@Delete`); `backend/src/notes/notes.controller.ts:11, 21`
- **What:** The permission is declared once at class level and inherited by every route, including mutations. `update()` and `remove()` do not receive the caller at all (`activities.controller.ts:31, 36`), so no ownership check is possible.
- **Failure scenario:** A user holding only `activities:view` can delete any other user's activity.

#### [P1] Email sending is authenticated but has no authorization

- **Where:** `backend/src/emails/emails.controller.ts:8` — `@UseGuards(JwtAuthGuard)` only; no `PermissionGuard`, no `@RequirePermission`
- **Failure scenario:** Any logged-in account — including the most minimally privileged — can `POST /api/emails` and send arbitrary outbound mail through the company's configured SMTP, with tracking pixels, to any address. Reputational and phishing exposure, plus the outbound rate limit is only the global 100/min.

#### [P1] IDOR on mutation handlers of owner-bearing records

- **Where:** `expenses.service.ts:152`, `sales-orders.service.ts:154`, `purchase-orders.service.ts:163`, `vendor-bills.service.ts:195`, `tasks.service.ts:153`, `projects.service.ts:129`
- **What:** Update/delete/status/approval handlers fetch by raw id (`findUnique({ id })`) without intersecting caller scope — so even once §2.8's fail-open is fixed, mutations remain unscoped.

#### [P1] Payment mutations reuse document permissions

- **Where:** `invoices.controller.ts:77, 79`; `vendor-bills.controller.ts:63, 70`
- **What:** Recording an invoice payment requires only `invoices:create`; deleting a vendor-bill payment requires only `vendor-bills:edit`. There is no distinct payment permission, so anyone who can draft a document can move money against it.

#### [P1] Conversion endpoints don't check the target resource's permission

- **Where:** `quotes.controller.ts:63, 70`; `sales-orders.controller.ts:80`; `purchase-orders.controller.ts:74`
- **What:** Quote→invoice conversion is gated on `quotes:create`, not `invoices:create`. A user with no invoice permission at all can mint invoices by converting quotes.

#### [P1] Four enforced permissions cannot be granted to any role

- **Where:** `frontend/src/config/permissions.ts:1-20` vs `frontend/src/pages/main.tsx` and the backend guards
- **What:** Routes and controllers enforce `leads:view|create|edit`, `accounting:view`, `warehouses:view`
  and `activities:view`. `PERMISSION_REGISTRY` contains **no `leads`, `warehouses` or `activities` key at
  all**, and lists `accounting` as `["manage"]` only. The Roles admin UI builds its entire grid from
  `Object.keys(PERMISSION_REGISTRY)` (`frontend/src/components/Roles/Roles.tsx:68`).
- **Failure scenario:** An administrator cannot grant Leads access to a sales role, or read-only Accounting
  access to a bookkeeper, through the product. Only holders of `*` (superadmin) reach those modules —
  everyone else gets Access Denied, since `Protected` correctly fails closed
  (`frontend/src/components/common/Protected.tsx:20-25`). That the seed builder hardcodes exactly these
  strings (`backend/src/sample-data/sample-data.builder.ts:58, 70`) confirms they were intended to be grantable.
- **Fix sketch:** Add the missing resources/actions to the registry and derive it from a single shared
  source used by both the guard and the UI, so drift is impossible.

#### [P1] Vendor-bill self-approval is skipped when the approval workflow is disabled

- **Where:** `backend/src/procurement/vendor-bills.service.ts:262-263`
- **What:** Both the approver-role check and the self-approval block are gated on `enabled`, which
  `getApprovalConfig()` defaults to **false**. `purchase-orders.service.ts:261` applies the identical
  block **unconditionally** — the asymmetry is the proof this is unintended.
- **Failure scenario:** With vendor-bill approvals off (the default), one user holding
  `vendor-bills:create` + `:approve` can create a supplier, raise a bill against it, approve it, and make
  it payable with no second pair of eyes — on the money-out path.

#### [P2] Two inconsistent superadmin escape hatches

Some modules bypass SoD on `userRole !== 'super admin'` (`expenses`, `purchase-orders`, `sales-orders`,
`vendor-bills`), others on `!userPermissions.includes('*')` (`invoices`, `quotes`). A role named
`super admin` and the `*` permission are not the same thing; one consistent mechanism is needed.

#### [P2] Global rate limit is too aggressive for a dense ERP — **PROVEN**

- **Where:** `backend/src/app.module.ts:84` — `ThrottlerModule.forRoot([{ ttl: 60000, limit: 100 }])` with a global `ThrottlerGuard`.
- **Failure scenario:** Observed directly during this audit: an automated sweep of ~19 screens exhausted
  the budget and the session was forced back to the login page — 179 consecutive `429`s. A single dense
  ERP page fires many parallel API calls, and an entire office behind one NAT shares this per-IP budget.
- **Fix sketch:** Raise the global ceiling, key on user id rather than IP for authenticated traffic, and
  keep the strict per-route limits already present on `/auth` (`auth.controller.ts:30, 52`).

- **Zero console or page errors** across all 40 valid frames.
- **Strong list-view information architecture** — KPI summary strip, status tab filters, search, sortable
  headers, right-aligned numerics with muted currency suffixes, colour-coded status badges, and a global

**Two consequences worth separating:**

1. **(SC 1.4.11) Borders are effectively invisible — 1.18:1 to 1.31:1.** Confirmed visually in the dark

   scanning function. This is the single highest-value colour fix.

   buttons fail AA by a wide margin. Either darken the accent ramps or switch `--primary-foreground` to a
   dark token per accent.

### 3.3 Keyboard and semantics

- **(SC 4.1.2) Icon-only controls without accessible names.** Measured live: Chart of Accounts **24 of 40**
  buttons (the per-row edit/delete pencil and trash), Vendor Bills 14/27, Projects 14/29, and a consistent
  4 per row-action menu on every other list. Add `aria-label` in `RowActions` and the COA row controls —
  one shared fix covers most of the surface.
- **(SC 2.1.1) 10–14 clickable non-interactive elements per list page** carry `cursor-pointer` but no
  `role`, no `tabIndex` and no key handler — these are the clickable table rows. Keyboard and screen-reader
  users cannot open a record from the list. Fix once inside `GenericTable`.
- **(SC 3.3.2) Two unlabelled inputs on every list page** (the search field and the rows-per-page select).
- **(SC 2.4.7)** `outline-none` appears in 10+ components including `ui/button.tsx`, `ui/checkbox.tsx` and
  `GenericKanban.tsx`; confirm each has a replacement `focus-visible` ring.

### 3.4 Density and workflow efficiency

- **Default 10 rows per page is too low.** The Chart of Accounts — a reference structure users scan —
  is spread across **3 pages**. Roughly 300 px of the 900 px viewport is chrome before the first row.
  Raise the default to 25–50, add a density toggle, and virtualise long lists.
- **The Chart of Accounts is rendered as a flat list although the data model is a tree.**
  `ChartOfAccount` has `parentId` / `parent` / `children` with an index on `parentId`
  (`schema.prisma:1349-1351, 1364`), and the seeded codes are visibly hierarchical
  (1000 Assets → 1100 Cash & Bank → 1101 Main Cash Box). Rendering the existing hierarchy is a
  high-value, low-cost change.
- **(RTL bug) Mixed-direction text truncates on the wrong side.** In Arabic, English invoice titles
  render as `...oice for Birthday — Salma Zaki` — the ellipsis eats the _beginning_ of the string, which
  is the identifying part. Apply `dir="auto"` or `unicode-bidi: plaintext` to mixed-content cells.
- Column widths appear fixed rather than proportional: titles truncate while horizontal space is free.
